# Results review (Nov 2026): temperature-only vs temperature+precip, baselines, paper audit

Context document so a new session (or coauthor) can pick this up cold.
Written 2026-11-16. **Nothing described here is committed to git.**

## 1. Purpose of the project and of this review

MSN (Masked Siamese Network, ViT-Small, prototypes) clusters daily ERA5 Amazonia fields
(deseasonalised per-month, per-cell z-scores) into "regimes". The paper
([paper/main.tex](../paper/main.tex)) tests whether regime *occupancy* (monthly frequency) is linked to
ENSO (ONI index), using a circular-rotation null + max-statistic FWER.

The review asked: (1) do new runs (15 vs 30 prototypes, temperature-only, plus temperature+precip) change the
story; (2) are the paper's statistics correct and the claims supported; (3) which results to present;
(4) how dispersed are samples inside each cluster (QQ, dispersion stats, samples).

\*misfit = % of days whose temperature field is closer (Euclidean) to another cluster's composite.
eta² of precipitation (cbrt-z): MSN TP30 cbrt 10.1 %, raw 8.6 %, k-means (TP) 17–21 %, bins ~2.7 %.

Key reading:
- **Every partition, even 1-D quantile bins, has a significant omnibus ENSO test** (the monthly level already
  correlates with ONI at 0.42). The standard omnibus test cannot tell methods apart.
- **The level-partial test can**: bins lose all signal; MSN keeps it (p ≤ 0.01, 3–4 clusters) at a magnitude
  comparable to k-means. So MSN carries ENSO information beyond the level, but **not more than classical k-means**.
- ~85 % of what MSN separates is domain-mean level; pattern eta² is 7–11 % (k-means 17–21 %).
- Detrending *increases* FWER counts: trend is not inflating the ENSO links.

## 6. Other checks

- **Rotation null floor.** With free shifts of 1–6 months, shifted ONI still beats the observed omnibus
  statistic, so the paper's p = 0.013 is an artifact floor. With |shift| ≥ 12 or 24, no rotation beats it
  (p ≈ 0.002 = minimum possible) and FWER counts rise.
- **Citation.** Ebisuzaki (1997) is phase randomisation, not circular rotation.
- **Inference crop.** `Resize(146)+CenterCrop(128)` keeps only lon −69.3…−48.7, lat −16.6…4.1 (61 % of the
  domain). Full-field re-inference (`crop_test.py`): same cluster for 80 % (T15) / 71 % (T30) of days;
  T15 omnibus S 0.624 → 0.588, nFWER 5 → 5; T30 S 0.843 → 0.771, nFWER 7 → 5. Conclusions unchanged.
- **Seasonal-field variant** (paper): S = 0.377 vs anomaly 0.691 (~1.8× weaker; "about twice" roughly OK), but 4–5 FWER clusters.
- **Corrected ONI labels** (code is right): 117 El Niño months, 47 episodes, 11 events.
  Paper says 67 / 35 / 9, S = 0.485, split-half r = 0.78; current code on the paper model gives S = 0.691, split-half r = 0.83.
- **Precipitation (TP30).** cbrt vs raw: drought-year regime shift +15.9 pp vs +5.5 pp, flood +0.9 pp (p = 0.84) vs
  +4.1 pp; sign pattern flips after detrending (cbrt: drought +10.1 pp p = 0.002; raw: flood significant,
  drought ns). The trivial annual domain-mean precipitation discriminates drought years at p ≤ 0.001, and
  drought years overlap El Niño years, so the test is **not** independent validation. Best epochs
  74 (cbrt) / 53 (raw); val MSN worsens afterwards in both.
- **Why cbrt/transform:** tp near-zero is per cell/season, not per day (see memory note `precip-channel-diagnostics`).

## 8. Paper audit and recommendations

Issues in `paper/main.tex` / `make_figures.py` (based on the A100 T15 run):
1. Numbers outdated vs corrected labels (see §6); regenerate `stats.json`/figures.
2. Rotation null permits tiny shifts → p = 0.013 is a floor; use min shift ≥ 24 and exact p.
3. Ebisuzaki citation does not match the method.
4. Text says "eleven anchor views"; code uses 1 target view (128) + 10 anchor views (64).
5. "Independent fingerprint" is not independent (composite built from regimes over-represented in El Niño).
6. Effective window is 61 % of the domain unless full-field inference is used.
7. Training details (A100, 300 epochs) must match whichever run is reported.
8. One seed per configuration; BatchNorm sees micro-batch (128) stats ("ghost BN"); equal-size clusters are imposed.

Recommended content:
- **Main model:** T15 temperature-only (prefer the new, reproducible T15; cite the A100 model as replication).
- **Main results:** ENSO association with corrected null; **level vs pattern decomposition + level-partial test**;
  **baselines table (bins, k-means)**; replicable regimes (warm El Niño, cold La Niña, N–S dipole);
  between-training replication (r = 0.79).
- **Supplement:** T30 (more FWER clusters, better pattern separation, same story), crop and detrend robustness,
  one dispersion panel (SNR + samples).
- **Drop/soften:** "two flavours of El Niño", "independent fingerprint", any claim of beating classical methods.
  Drought/flood test only as a sanity check.
- **Precipitation (TP30):** not in the main paper (η² of precip 8.6–10 % vs 14–21 % k-means; drought/flood result
  depends on the transform); appendix/future work with the caveat above.

## 13. Two-level regimes: level first, then pattern within warm / cold days (2026-11-21)

`scripts/review/hierarchical_warm.py` (outputs `docs/review_2026-11/hierarchical*/`). t30_nomean_200ep (fresh
200-epoch run, job 172, best epoch 89) is equivalent to the 100-epoch run (η² pattern 16.8 vs 16.6 %, same ENSO
links), so longer training does not help.

**Trend.** The per-month z-scored anomalies still contain the warming trend (+0.15–0.17 σ/decade domain mean):
without detrending, "warm days" are mostly 2016–2025 and "cold days" the 1980s. With a per-pixel linear trend
removed (`DETREND=1`), the warm years are 2024, 1998, 1987, 1983, 2016, 2023, 2010, 2015.
TNA index: NOAA PSL `tna.data` (in `../data/indices/`). Detrended monthly ONI–TNA corr: +0.18.

**Method.** Level 1: top / bottom 20 % of the daily domain-mean T anomaly (train-day thresholds). Level 2:
k-means within those days on (a) EOFs of the pattern field (T minus its daily domain mean, ± precip) or (b) the
self-supervised t30_nomean embedding. Test of the second level = **composition**: in months with ≥ 5 warm days,
the share of each sub-regime among them, partial r with ONI (controlling TNA) and with TNA (controlling ONI),
all detrended, exact rotation null (|shift| ≥ 24), FWER over sub-regimes.

**Results (detrended).**
- MSN embedding, warm days: an "El Niño heat" sub-regime (hot and dry north/central Amazon, wet south) whose
  share rises with ONI (partial r +0.22..+0.26, p ≤ 0.04) and a "non-ENSO heat" sub-regime (uniform mild
  warmth, no rain anomaly) whose share falls (−0.22..−0.30). Holds for K = 3, 4, 6, for quantiles 20 % and 30 %
  (10 %: same signs, p 0.06–0.12), and with the independent 200-epoch embedding. Not related to TNA.

## 14. Level control, ENSO fingerprint beyond the regional mean, detrended training plan (2026-11-21)

Notation. Channel c ∈ {Tmin, Tmax, P}, pixel (i, j), day d, calendar month m(d), time t in decades.
Anomaly (as in training): z_cij(d) = (x_cij(d) − μ_cij,m(d)) / σ_cij,m(d), statistics on train years
(x = cbrt(tp) for P). Detrended: z̃_cij(d) = z_cij(d) − (a_cij + b_cij·t_d), OLS on train days only.
Level: L(d) = mean over c ∈ {Tmin, Tmax}, i, j of z̃_cij(d). Pattern: z̃_cij(d) − mean_ij z̃_c(d).

### 14.2 Is there an ENSO pattern that a regional mean cannot carry? (`enso_fingerprint.py`, `fingerprint/`)

No clustering. Monthly means of z̃ (detrended per pixel, train months). For each pixel:
raw r_ij = pcorr(X_ij, ONI | TNA); pattern R_ij = X_ij − (α_ij + β_ij·L_T) (precip: also − γ_ij·L_P),
r_ij^pat = pcorr(R_ij, ONI | TNA, L). All series detrended. Field significance: exact rotation of ONI
(|shift| ≥ 24): share of area with |r| > 1.96/√n, and max |r|. Per season and ONI lag 0 / 3.

| season | var | r(level, ONI) | raw: area sig | pattern: area sig (p) | pattern: max abs r (p) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| all | T | +0.56 | 100 % | 49 % (0.002) | 0.58 (0.002) |
| all | P | −0.30 | 53 % | 58 % (0.002) | 0.32 (0.002) |
| DJF | T | +0.80 | 100 % | 19 % (0.15) | 0.51 (0.002) |
| JJA | T | +0.34 | 56 % | 32 % (0.010) | 0.64 (0.002) |
| JJA | P | −0.05 | 49 % | 48 % (0.002) | 0.54 (0.002) |
| SON | T | +0.37 | 82 % | 44 % (0.004) | 0.68 (0.002) |
| SON | P | −0.30 | 46 % | 35 % (0.030) | 0.41 (0.026) |

(full table incl. MAM and lag 3 in `fingerprint/fingerprint.md`.) **Yes: ENSO has a significant spatial
fingerprint beyond the regional mean**: relative to the domain mean, El Niño warms the north/central Amazon more
and the northeast corner and the south less, and dries the north while wetting the south (a north–south rainfall
dipole). It moves with the season (north in JJA, central-east in SON) and is strongest in JJA/SON, when the
domain-mean link is weakest; in DJF ENSO is almost all level. The level carries most variance (mean r² 0.16 vs
0.02), but locally the beyond-mean signal reaches |r| 0.5–0.7. This fingerprint is the target a clustering
method must recover to claim it shows "what a regional mean cannot".

### 14.3 Why the daily clusters only partly find it

ENSO acts on monthly-to-seasonal scales; a daily field is dominated by synoptic weather (cold surges, rain
bands). Daily clustering therefore mostly finds weather types; ENSO only shifts their frequencies, and the
detectable part of that shift is mostly the level. The fingerprint is a low-frequency, beyond-level signal.

### 14.4 Detrended training (proposed, not run)

Per pixel and channel, after the climatology z-score, on train days only:
(a, b)_cij = argmin Σ_d∈train (z_cij(d) − a − b·t_d)²; z̃ = z − a − b·t (validation extrapolates the line).
Options: one slope per pixel (default) or one per pixel × calendar month (warming differs by season); linear is
conservative (a smoother trend would absorb decadal ENSO-like variability). Then the pattern input
z̃ − mean_ij z̃ (the `remove_spatial_mean` step). Implementation: a `detrend` option in `Era5Dataset` next to
`deseason_standardize`, coefficients stored with the climatology, re-export the .npy. Note that removing the
domain mean already removes the spatially uniform part of the trend, so detrending alone will change the
pattern embedding little; the larger expected gain is the **time scale** (14.5).

### 14.5 Plan to get patterns the regional mean cannot show

1. Input = detrended **pattern** fields (T and P jointly) at the ENSO time scale: 30-day running means of z̃
   (≈ 16 k overlapping samples, enough for MSN; monthly alone has 552).
2. First with PCA + k-means locally (hours), then MSN on the A100 (2–3 seeds) only if the k-means version works.
3. Success criteria: (i) occupancy vs ONI significant after controlling L (partial test); (ii) the ENSO-linked
   clusters' composites match the 14.2 fingerprint (spatial correlation); (iii) more than one ENSO-linked type
   that differ in pattern, not only intensity (e.g. Pacific vs Atlantic forcing, eastern vs central Pacific El
   Niño, seasonal position of the dipole), tested with the level-controlled composition test (14.1).
4. Robustness: RONI (NOAA CPC operational since 2026-02-01, relative Niño 3.4) besides ONI; lags 0–3; seasons.

### 14.6 Reference papers and how they compare

- Polifke (2026, Clim. Dyn. 64:495): GPCC daily 1°, 1982–2020, aggregated to monthly totals; anomaly
  P' = P − P̄_m (1982–2010 climatology) and percent anomaly P% = 100·P'/P̄_m; cos-lat area means over three fixed
  boxes (Amazon 15°S–5°N, 75–50°W; NE Brazil; S Brazil/La Plata); DJF and SON only; **no detrending** (early vs
  late subperiod comparison instead); indices z-scored on 1982–2010 (Niño 3.4 in °C, ±0.5); lags 0–3; block
  bootstrap (3-month blocks), BH-FDR; additive vs interaction regression (no FDR-significant interaction);
  year-grouped out-of-fold ML (Amazon OOF R² ≈ 0.2). For us: keep cbrt-z for modelling (percent anomalies blow
  up in the dry season at daily scale), but report composites also as monthly mm and % anomalies so results are
  comparable; adopt seasons, lags, FDR maps and the subperiod check.
- van Oldenborgh et al. (2021, ERL 16 044003): relative Niño 3.4 (minus tropical-mean SST) because warming
  inflates El Niño and deflates La Niña in the standard index; basis of NOAA's RONI.
- Jiménez-Muñoz et al. (2016, Sci. Rep.): 2015–16 Amazon heat = similar El Niño contribution to 1982–83 and
  1997–98 plus the warming trend; the trend/ENSO confound we found.
- Earth's Future (2026, incl. A. Taschetto): hot-dry extremes in six Amazon sub-regions, monthly 1950–2023;
  ENSO leads by up to 7 months, the tropical Atlantic adds information in some regions/seasons. Closest to our
  question but with fixed regions; to read and cite.

### 14.7 Research questions (draft)

Q1. Beyond the regional-mean warming/drying, does ENSO have a spatial fingerprint over Amazonia, and how does it
move with the season and lag? (14.2: yes.)
Q2. Can an unsupervised method (self-supervised MSN or PCA + k-means) discover regimes that carry this
fingerprint, without being told about ENSO?
Q3. Are there several ENSO-related regime types that differ in pattern (not only intensity), and do they map
to Pacific vs Atlantic forcing or ENSO flavours?
Q4. Are temperature and rainfall effects additive (as Polifke finds for climate modes)? (§12: yes.)

### 14.8 Status for a first paper

Supported now: Q1 (fingerprint), Q4 (additivity), regime occupancy predicts ONI (R² ≈ 0.3), the La Niña cold vs
friagem split surviving level control, the trend confound and its fix. Not yet supported: Q3 for warm days (the

### 14.9 Decision (user, 2026-11-21): climate types first, ENSO second; no daily-mean removal

- Do **not** remove the daily domain mean from the input: the anomaly z̃ (deseasonalised and detrended) is
  the field; removing L(d) makes days harder to compare and interpret. The `nomean` runs stay as a
  sensitivity check only.
- Goal reframed: find **distinct climate types** (heat type A, heat type B, ..., cold types, wet/dry types) with
  no ENSO information, describe them, and only then relate their frequency to ENSO. Distinct ENSO patterns are
  not the target. Intensity is part of a type's identity, so "El Niño favours type A" is a valid result even if
  A is also the more intense type; the level-controlled test (14.1) is reported as a secondary decomposition
  (how much of the link is intensity), not as a requirement.
- Requirements for the types: different spatial structure, not scaled copies of one map (pattern correlation of
  normalised composites), stable across seeds, physically describable (season, persistence, T and P).
- Next analysis: clustering of z̃ (T + P, full field), all days and within warm/cold days; describe types;
  then monthly frequency vs ONI/RONI and TNA, by season and lag 0–3; lift in El Niño / La Niña months.
